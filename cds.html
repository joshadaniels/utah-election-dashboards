Turnout = ballots reported ÷ total active registered voters statewide (1,784,255, Nov 3 voted file). “Estimated outstanding” is the sum of the counties’ yet-to-report figures (each county’s estimated total received − ballots in results). “Likely to count” strips out ballots the counties have already counted but the state results file hasn’t published yet (Summary PDF header gaps) and ballots expected to be rejected — see the All Counties page for each county’s inputs.

Est. left is built per county, the same method as the legislative districts: each CD’s expected total in a county is a blended share of that county’s estimated received (see the Closest Legislative Contests page for the formula), minus what it has counted there — then the CDs in each county are scaled so they sum exactly to that county’s remaining pool. The pool is the likely-to-count pool (raw yet-to-report minus header-gap ballots minus expected rejections); the raw figure is shown under the likely figure in the same cell. CDs use the registration share only (100% current): the 2025 congressional redraw changed CD boundaries, so 2022/2024 CD shares are for different districts and are not comparable — so the CD low–high range collapses to the point. “Est. received” is ballots in results + raw est. left (no county report exists at CD level). “Registered” is active registered voters as of Nov 3.

How to read this: same definitions as the Closest Legislative Contests page. “Could still flip” means the trailing candidate’s needed share of the district’s estimated outstanding ballots is within a plausible range — estimated from the district’s partisan registration (active registered voters, Tableau voter file) plus how much that party’s vote over- or underperformed its registration in the congressional contest in that county, with the range from how much that over/underperformance varied across the CD’s counties, and a county-weighted counted-share check (see the Closest Legislative Contests page for the full method). “Expected to flip” means the expected share alone is enough. “Too close” means the trailing candidate could still overtake (the district’s raw remaining ballots exceed the leader’s margin); “Final” means the margin exceeds that maximum, so the outcome cannot change. Canvassed counties report no further ballots, so a district’s outstanding count covers only its uncanvassed counties. “Flipped” compares the leader’s party to the party that held that district number before the 2025 congressional redraw (Moore CD1, Maloy CD2, Kennedy CD3, Owens CD4) — the redraw moved the incumbents, so this is a district-number comparison, not same-boundaries.
