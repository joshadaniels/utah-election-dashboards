Methodology — how turnout is measured here

There is no single turnout rate. The same election produces very different rates depending on the denominator, so this page shows four of them side by side, each labeled with its denominator, following the framework of McDonald & Popkin (2001) and the MIT Election Data + Science Lab. The scholarly consensus ranks the denominators voting-eligible population (VEP) > citizen voting-age population (CVAP) > voting-age population (VAP) > registered voters for comparing turnout over time. No county-level VEP exists, so the county headline is ballots cast per CVAP; the statewide headline is the UF Election Lab’s VEP series, adopted as published.

What each series is and is not. Official turnout is the rate the state publishes; it moves when registration law and list maintenance change, not just when voting changes. CVAP turnout is the best county-level citizen denominator, but CVAP is a survey estimate with a margin of error, not a count. VAP turnout includes noncitizens in the denominator, so it runs lower than CVAP turnout and drifts down as the noncitizen share grows. VEP turnout is the preferred headline measure but exists only statewide — the felony and overseas components do not exist at county level, so no county VEP is attempted.

Registered voters: active vs total

Utah’s official turnout uses active registered voters only. The canvasses say so explicitly from 2012 on (“ACTIVE REGISTERED VOTERS” / “Total Active Registered” / “Active voters”). Before 2012 the canvass column reads simply “REGISTERED VOTERS” with no qualifier; per the state’s own Historical Voter Turnout sheet, pre-2012 figures “may include active and inactive voters.” A turnout series crossing 2012 is therefore not apples-to-apples on the denominator — part of any 2010–2012 move is a definition change, not a behavior change. The 2022 canvass header is again unqualified (“Registered Voters”); the figure is consistent with active registration (total registered was ~1.9M+), but the canvass does not state the definition. Each row’s table entry shows the definition as printed.

CVAP: a five-year estimate with a margin of error

CVAP comes from the Census Bureau’s special tabulation of the American Community Survey. Each vintage pools 60 months of responses, so it describes the average citizen voting-age population over a five-year period, centered roughly 2.5 years before the vintage’s end year — it is not a count of who lived in a county on Election Day, and it lags fast-growing counties (Utah, Washington, Tooele). Rates computed as ballots ÷ CVAP are estimates: the chart shades the 90% margin-of-error band wherever the Census Bureau publishes an MOE (2008–2026). In the smallest counties the MOE is large (Daggett 2020–2024: 610 ± 87, 14.3%; Piute 1,356 ± 101, 7.4%), and CVAP-based rates can exceed 100% in small or fast-growing counties — that is sampling error and period lag, not a data error. The 2000 CVAP is decennial long-form sample data (Census 2000 PHC-T-31) and publishes no MOE.

Why the CVAP line is flat in 2022–2026

The latest CVAP vintage is the 2020–2024 ACS 5-year release (confirmed at census.gov/rdo, Nov 19, 2026; no 2021–2025 CVAP exists yet — the 2021–2025 ACS 5-year estimates are not due until Dec 2026). The 2022, 2024, and 2026 rows therefore all reuse that one vintage and are identical by construction. Do not read the flat tail as “no population growth” — it is a data-availability limit. The 2024 and 2026 rows are flagged, and both should be re-pointed at the 2021–2025 vintage when it releases (expected early 2027).

Primaries and the SB 54 break

Primary turnout is not comparable across 2014. Before SB 54 (2014), Utah primaries happened only when no candidate cleared 60% at the party convention, and the set of offices on the primary ballot varied by year and county. SB 54 added a signature path to the ballot, which changed what a primary is. The primary chart marks the break prominently. Primary denominators are also not one consistent definition: the 1996 denominator covers only precincts that held a primary (Weber 1996: 9,490 registered voters); the 2020 Utah County figures are Republican-only; the 2020 Salt Lake denominator mixes active Republican voters, active Democratic voters in two districts, and unaffiliated voters in selected school-board districts; the 2024 Salt Lake and Weber figures are the Republican statewide table only (active as of June 20, 2024), while 2024 Utah and Davis include Republican plus nonpartisan school-board primary ballots. Each row’s note preserves its definition. Do not chart primary turnout as a single apples-to-apples series. (Utah held presidential primaries in 2008, 2020, and 2024; 2012 and 2016 were caucuses, which have no primary turnout.)

Pre-2005 CVAP is modeled (experimental)

No CVAP product exists before the 2005–2009 ACS. For 1990–1998 and 2002–2006, county CVAP is modeled as county citizen share of VAP × July 1 PEP intercensal VAP, with the share interpolated between decennial anchors (the McDonald method, applied at county level): the 1990 anchor comes from the 1990 Census long form (CP-2 Table 138, all-ages citizenship, age-adjusted with the county’s 2000 age factor); the 2000 anchor is Census 2000 PHC-T-31; the 2007 anchor share comes from the 2005–2009 ACS. Modeled rows carry no MOE and are drawn dashed and labeled experimental. The 2005–2009 ACS vintage is deliberately not used as the 2006 value: it was the last vintage controlled to Census 2000-based population estimates, which badly overestimated Utah’s fast-growing counties (+5.2% statewide, +13.9% in Utah County vs the interpolated 2006 value), and it is centered a year after the 2006 election; using it would show a spurious 2006–2008 CVAP decline. The unused ACS value is recorded in every 2006 row’s note, so the choice is auditable and reversible.

What is provisional in 2026

The 2026 general election is provisional throughout. San Juan County had not canvassed as of Nov 19, 2026 (canvass completes ~Nov 23): its ballots figure (5,146) is the state results feed’s reported figure and its registered-voter count is null, so the statewide registered total (1,776,347) covers 28 counties only. The 2026 population and VAP figures are extrapolated 16 months beyond the latest published July 1 estimate (Vintage 2025, through July 1, 2025) using each county’s 2024–2025 annual change; they will be replaced when Vintage 2026 releases (~mid-2027). There is no 2026 VEP row: the UF Election Lab has not published a certified-data 2026 release (its preliminary file predates the election and carries a round pre-certification estimate for Utah ballots counted). Cache County’s 2026 figures come from the county Summary Results PDF (“Registered Voters – Total”) rather than the canvass statistics form, and Utah County’s active-voter figure (374,971) is the certified canvass figure — the county re-posted the stats file Nov 18 with 374,970 (both round to 51.93%).
