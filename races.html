How to read this: “Margin %” is the leader’s lead over the runner-up as a share of the top-two vote. “Margin at first report” is the same figure in the Nov 4 noon release; “Δ margin” is the change since then in percentage points (negative = the race tightened). “Flipped” means the leading candidate’s party differs from the party of the incumbent who held the seat. “Could still flip” means the trailing candidate would need under 60% of the district’s POINT estimated outstanding ballots to overtake — i.e. a plausible, not just mathematical, path on ballots left to count. The district’s outstanding is built per county from a blended share of that county’s estimated received (30% the district’s share of the county’s active registered voters, 50% its share of the county’s 2022 ballots, 20% its share of the county’s 2024 ballots; Senate districts not up in 2024 use 40/60 current/2022), minus counted, reconciled per county so the districts in each county sum to that county’s remaining pool. The parenthetical range is the low–high from the spread of those components (a year’s share is dropped if that year’s race was unopposed; CDs use the registration share only, since the 2025 redraw makes 2022/2024 CD shares non-comparable). Where the range collapses to a single number, the district has no usable historical spread (unopposed in both available years, or a CD). See back-data-method.md for the full method. “Too close” is a margin under 2%. All figures are unofficial until canvassed.
