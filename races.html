How to read this: “Margin %” is the leader’s lead over the runner-up as a share of the top-two vote. “Margin at first report” is the same figure in the Nov 4 noon release; “Δ margin” is the change since then in percentage points (negative = the race tightened). “Flipped” means the leading candidate’s party differs from the party of the incumbent who held the seat. “Could still flip” means the trailing candidate’s needed share of the district’s estimated outstanding ballots is within a plausible range — the range is estimated from the district’s partisan registration (active registered voters, Tableau voter file) plus how much that party’s vote over- or underperformed its registration in the overlapping congressional race in that county, with the range taken from how much that over/underperformance varied across the overlapping CD’s counties. The “Trailer’s share of uncounted” column shows what the trailer needs, what share is expected, and the plausible maximum; “Expected to flip” means the expected share alone is enough. A county-weighted counted-share check keeps the estimate honest: the ceiling is never below what the trailer is actually getting in the counties where the uncounted ballots are, and where a candidate’s support is candidate-driven rather than party-driven (e.g. a minor-party trailer), the counted share replaces the party estimate. The district’s outstanding is built per county from a blended share of that county’s estimated received (30% the district’s share of the county’s active registered voters, 50% its share of the county’s 2022 ballots, 20% its share of the county’s 2024 ballots; Senate districts not up in 2024 use 40/60 current/2022), minus counted, reconciled per county so the districts in each county sum to that county’s remaining pool. The parenthetical range is the low–high from the spread of those components (a year’s share is dropped if that year’s race was unopposed; CDs use the registration share only, since the 2025 redraw makes 2022/2024 CD shares non-comparable). Where the range collapses to a single number, the district has no usable historical spread (unopposed in both available years, or a CD). See back-data-method.md and back-data/flip-model-method.md for the full methods. “Too close” is a margin under 2%. All figures are unofficial until canvassed.
